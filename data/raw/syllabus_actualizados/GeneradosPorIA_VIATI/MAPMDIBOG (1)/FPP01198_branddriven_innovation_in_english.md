## 3. Descripción de la Unidad de Estudio

This unit of study focuses on the strategic management of brand identity as the core driver for corporate innovation and product development. In the context of 1967's rapidly expanding consumer markets and the rise of multinational corporations, the course explores the integration of classic "brand management" systems with emerging theories of consumer behavior and systematic product planning. Taught entirely in English, it prepares postgraduate students to lead brand-driven innovation initiatives, utilizing contemporary marketing research techniques, quantitative analysis, and media planning. (Presente en planes: MAPMDIBOG001 · MAPMDIBOG002)

## 4. Resultado de Aprendizaje de la Unidad

Diseña estrategias de innovación de producto y posicionamiento de marca fundamentadas en el análisis del comportamiento del consumidor, aplicando modelos cuantitativos de mercadeo y sistemas de gestión de marca para asegurar la ventaja competitiva en mercados locales y globales.

## 5. Contenidos de la Unidad de Estudio

### Por semestre (18 semanas)

*   **Week 1-3: Foundations of Brand Management and Product Innovation**
    *   The evolution of the "Brand Man" system (Procter & Gamble classic model).
    *   Defining brand equity and corporate image in the late 1960s.
    *   The role of innovation in corporate growth and survival.
*   **Week 4-6: Consumer Behavior and Market Segmentation**
    *   Motivation research and the psychological aspects of consumer choice (Ernest Dichter's theories).
    *   Demographic and psychographic segmentation methods.
    *   Cultural influences on brand adoption in emerging Latin American markets.
*   **Week 7-9: Product Life Cycle and Systematic Innovation**
    *   Theodore Levitt's Product Life Cycle (PLC) management.
    *   Diffusion of Innovations theory (Everett Rogers) and targeting early adopters.
    *   New product development processes: screening, testing, and commercialization.
*   **Week 10-12: Marketing Research and Quantitative Tools**
    *   Data-driven marketing: tabulating market research survey data.
    *   Linear programming applications for media selection and marketing budget allocation.
    *   Designing effective consumer surveys and panels.
*   **Week 13-15: Brand Positioning and Advertising Strategy**
    *   The Unique Selling Proposition (USP) vs. The Brand Image concept (David Ogilvy's approach).
    *   Media planning: coordinating television, radio, print, and direct mail.
    *   Measuring brand awareness and advertising effectiveness.
*   **Week 16-18: Final Applied Project and Case Studies**
    *   Analysis of Harvard Business School case studies on international brand launchings.
    *   Group presentations and defense of the Brand-Driven Innovation Proposal.
